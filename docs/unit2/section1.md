#Unit 2, Section 1: Compare-a-Chutes

**<h1>Section 1: Compare-a-Chutes</h1>**

##<p style="background: #00869b; color: white; text-align: left; padding: 10px;">**Overarching Themes**</p>

This section shifts students from describing a single distribution to comparing two, through the lens of Engineers testing parachute designs. By moving through every phase of The Data Cycle, students discover that variability is unavoidable: two parachutes built from identical materials and dimensions still produce different descent times, and the same parachute rarely lands at exactly the same time twice. A central focus is placed on careful, standardized data collection (the “Rules of the Drop”) so that differences between distributions can be traced to the design change rather than uncontrolled factors. Students then weigh the evidence using stacked boxplots in CODAP, along with measures of center (mean and median) and spread (MAD and range). Ultimately, the curriculum emphasizes that a verdict is only as strong as its evidence: a difference between two groups is meaningful only when it is large compared to the natural variability within each group.
