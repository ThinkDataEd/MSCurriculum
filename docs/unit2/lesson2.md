##**<u>Lesson 2: Construct-a-Chute</u>**

###**Objective:**
Students will construct a modified parachute and collect precise descent time data to prepare for statistical comparison.

###**Materials:**

1. Materials for parachute construction – enough for each group to construct 1 parachute:

    100. Tissue paper or plastic bags

    100. Scissors

    100. Ruler

    100. Tape

    100. 1-hole punch

    100. String, yarn, twine or fishing line

    100. Weighted item of approximately 4.5-5.5 grams (examples: small non-breakable action figure, Lego mini-figure, 25 mm binder clip or metal washers)

    100. Location from which to drop a parachute from the same height.

2. Stopwatches – one per group

3. Tape measures – one per group

4. How to Make a Parachute handout ([LMR_U2_L01_A_How_to_Make_a_Parachute](../MSDS_Curriculum/2_MSDS_LMRs/MSDS_LMR_Unit_2/LMR_U2_L01_A.pdf)) – one copy per group

5. Parachute Modification Descriptions file ([LMR_U2_L02_A_Parachute_Modification_Descriptions](../MSDS_Curriculum/2_MSDS_LMRs/MSDS_LMR_Unit_2/LMR_U2_L02_A.pdf))
     ***<span style="background: #d9f0d3; color: black;">Advanced preparation required.</span>*** *See Class Setup section for additional details.*

6. Compare-a-Chute Descent Times handout ([LMR U2_L02_B_Compare-a-Chute_Descent_Times](../MSDS_Curriculum/2_MSDS_LMRs/MSDS_LMR_Unit_2/LMR_U2_L02_B.pdf)) – one copy per group

###**Vocabulary:**
n/a

###**Essential Concepts:**

!!! note "Essential Concepts: "
    Accurate data collection is important to ensure that differences between distributions are due to the independent variable rather than uncontrolled factors.

8. As a class, review the “Rules of the Drop” from [Lesson 1](lesson1.md). Things to consider include:

    100. When does the timer start – *typically when the parachute is released.*

    100. When does the timer end – *typically when the payload touches the ground.*

    100. From what height is the parachute dropped – *varies depending on what options are available. Suggestions for drop locations include from balconies, in stairwells if open enough, from the top of playground equipment like a slide, from a stage, or from gymnasium or stadium bleachers.*

    100. Mechanism for the drop – *suggestions include finger grip, clothespins, chip clips or binder clips.*

9. Answer any questions students have about the task and then provide time for groups to perform 20 successful drops and record the needed data. Remind groups that if the parachute hits a wall, gets tangled, or the timer misses the start or landing, the trial is to be discarded and repeated. Stress consistency over speed.

    <center><h3>Closing</h3></center>

10. As groups finish, have them review their data. Ask them to look for their minimum and maximum times and whether they look like they belong or if they appear to be possible extreme values.

11. Discussion: Engage in a class discussion around standardization. Ask students to look at the times for the first 5 drops and compare them to the times for the last 5 drops and ask if the first 5 times seem as accurate as the last 5 times. Discuss how teams may have gotten more standardized as they practiced.

12. Transition: Ensure all groups have a table with 20 descent times ready for analyzing with CODAP in the next lesson.

13. Exit Ticket: Consider the original data collected for Parachute P1 and answer the following questions: 
Based on the raw numbers you collected today for the drop times of your assigned re-engineered parachute, how do you think your data will compare to the data from Parachute P1? 
What is one specific reason, either from the design or the drop technique, that explains why you expect this result?
